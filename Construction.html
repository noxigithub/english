The "Apply" vs. "Install" Rule

In construction documentation, verb choice affects the scope of work and technical meaning. Translating these accurately is critical.

APPLY (Aplicar)

Use for: Liquids, Coatings, and Thin Materials.

Used when a material goes onto a surface and becomes part of it (e.g., brushing, rolling, or spraying).

Examples: Sealer, liquid waterproofing, stain, window film, fireproofing.

INSTALL (Instalar)

Use for: Systems, Components, and Assemblies.

Used when positioning, connecting, or assembling manufactured pieces that maintain their individual identity.

Examples: Doors, HVAC systems, plumbing fixtures, roof insulation, steel beams.

⚠️ The Exception: Sealant (Sellador de juntas)

Sealant is highly technical and involves joint preparation, backer rods, and tooling. Therefore, in English construction standards, sealant is installed, not applied.

Common Site Expressions

  • "Let’s check the floor plan layout." ➔ "Revisemos el diseño del plano de planta." (Used when verifying the exact location of a wall or fixture).
  • "Are we providing and installing this system?" ➔ "¿Nosotros suministramos e instalamos este sistema?" (Used to clarify if a specific trade is responsible for both buying the material and putting it in place).
  • "Has the surface been prepped for the finish?" ➔ "¿Se ha preparado la superficie para el acabado?" (Asking if drywall or concrete is ready for paint or sealer).
  • "We need to coordinate the trades." ➔ "Necesitamos coordinar los oficios/contratistas." (Discussing the scheduling of plumbers, electricians, and framers so they don't block each other's work).
