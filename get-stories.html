🚀 Advanced Challenge: GET & USED TO

Story 1: The Expat Life

Arthur used to live in a quiet, sunny village in Spain. Last month, he relocated to London for a new job. During his first week, the transition was incredibly difficult. He got lost (get) almost every single day in the underground subway. The weather was another problem; it was getting colder (to be + get) rapidly, and Arthur wasn't used to the freezing rain.

However, time passed. Arthur is getting used to carrying an umbrella everywhere he goes. He gets (get - receive) a lot of support from his new colleagues, which helps immensely. While he still misses his hometown, he is used to the fast-paced city life now, and he finally got his apartment decorated (get causative).

Brain Check

Comprehension 1 Where did Arthur live before moving to London?
Comprehension 2 Why was his first week so difficult in terms of transportation?
Comprehension 3 What item does Arthur now carry with him all the time?
Reflection 1 Have you ever moved to a new city or country? What was the hardest thing to get used to?
Reflection 2 Why do you think the support from colleagues is important when someone is getting used to a new environment?
Teacher's Vocabulary Notes
  • Used to live: Past habit (Solía vivir).
  • Got lost: Become/Change of state (Se perdió).
  • Was getting colder: Continuous process of change (Se estaba poniendo más frío).
  • Wasn't used to: State of not being accustomed (No estaba acostumbrado a).
  • Is getting used to: The process of becoming accustomed (Se está acostumbrando a).

Story 2: The Tech Bootcamp

Leo used to work as a traditional graphic designer, but he wanted a career change. He enrolled in an intensive 12-week coding bootcamp. In the beginning, the programming logic got really complicated (get). He was getting frustrated (to be + get) every time his code threw an error, and he even thought he might get kicked out (passive get) of the program.

Leo didn't use to spend so many hours staring at a terminal screen. But slowly, things started to click. Now, he is getting used to debugging complex software problems. The pressure is high, but he is entirely used to the stress of tight deadlines. Yesterday, he finally got (get - obtain) his first job offer as a junior developer!

Brain Check

Comprehension 1 What was Leo's profession before the coding bootcamp?
Comprehension 2 How did Leo feel when his code threw an error?
Comprehension 3 What did Leo finally achieve at the end of the story?
Reflection 1 If you were to change careers suddenly, what do you think you would struggle to get used to?
Reflection 2 Do you think frustration is a necessary part of the learning process when getting used to a new skill?
Teacher's Vocabulary Notes
  • Used to work: Past habit (Solía trabajar).
  • Was getting frustrated: Process of becoming (Se estaba frustrando).
  • Get kicked out: Passive voice with Get (Ser expulsado).
  • Didn't use to: Negative past habit (No solía).
  • Is getting used to: Adapting to something new (Se está acostumbrando).

Story 3: The Marathon Runner

Sarah never used to exercise. In fact, she used to avoid any physical activity at all costs. But after turning 30, she decided to train for a marathon. The first few weeks were brutal. She got injured (passive get) during her second run because she didn't stretch properly. Her muscles were getting sore (to be + get) every single day.

Despite the pain, she didn't quit. She got (get - buy/obtain) better running shoes and hired a coach. Fast forward three months: Sarah is getting used to waking up at 5:00 AM for her morning runs. She is fully used to the physical exhaustion. Next month, she hopes to get to (get - arrive) the finish line.

Brain Check

Comprehension 1 What was Sarah's attitude towards exercise before she turned 30?
Comprehension 2 Why did Sarah get injured during her second run?
Comprehension 3 What two things did she do to improve her training situation?
Reflection 1 Have you ever tried to establish a difficult physical routine? How did you get used to it?
Reflection 2 Why is it so hard to break habits we are already used to (like avoiding exercise)?
Teacher's Vocabulary Notes
  • Never used to: Emphasized negative past habit (Nunca solía).
  • Got injured: Passive action (Se lesionó).
  • Were getting sore: Progressive change (Se estaban poniendo doloridos).
  • Is fully used to: Completely accustomed (Está completamente acostumbrada).
  • Get to: Arrive at a destination (Llegar a).

Story 4: The Rural Escape

David and Elena used to live in a noisy apartment in the center of New York. They were used to hearing police sirens and traffic all night. Seeking peace, they bought a small farm in the countryside. Instantly, they realized farm life is tough. On the very first day, their truck got stuck (passive get) in the mud.

Out there, it gets dark (get - become) very early, and the silence was deafening. Elena was getting anxious (to be + get) without the city lights. However, as the seasons change, they are getting used to the rhythm of nature. Now, they get (get - receive) fresh eggs every morning, and they realize they wouldn't trade this life for anything.

Brain Check

Comprehension 1 What kind of noises were David and Elena used to hearing at night?
Comprehension 2 What happened to their truck on the first day?
Comprehension 3 Why was Elena getting anxious in the countryside initially?
Reflection 1 Could you get used to living in complete silence in the countryside? Why or why not?
Reflection 2 Do you think humans are getting too used to the noise and chaos of modern cities?
Teacher's Vocabulary Notes
  • Were used to: Past state of being accustomed (Estaban acostumbrados a).
  • Got stuck: Passive situation/Accident (Se quedó atascado).
  • Gets dark: Change of state/environment (Se oscurece).
  • Was getting anxious: Developing a feeling (Se estaba poniendo ansiosa).
  • Are getting used to: Adapting to the environment (Se están acostumbrando a).

Story 5: The Language Learner

When Kenji first started learning English, he used to struggle a lot with pronunciation. He wasn't used to making the "th" sound, and it got (get - become) very frustrating for him. Whenever he tried to speak in class, he would get nervous (get - become) and forget his vocabulary.

His teacher told him, "Don't worry, your brain is just getting rewired (to be + get passive)." Kenji decided to practice in front of a mirror every day. Currently, he is getting used to the complex grammar rules. He is much more confident now and recently got accepted (passive get) into a university exchange program in Canada!

Brain Check

Comprehension 1 What specific sound was Kenji not used to making?
Comprehension 2 What happened to Kenji when he tried to speak in class?
Comprehension 3 What great news did Kenji receive recently regarding his studies?
Reflection 1 What is the hardest grammar rule or sound you have had to get used to in English?
Reflection 2 How does consistent practice help us get used to things that initially make us nervous?
Teacher's Vocabulary Notes
  • Used to struggle: Past habit of having difficulty (Solía tener dificultades).
  • Got frustrating: Became frustrating (Se volvió frustrante).
  • Is getting rewired: Passive continuous change (Se está reconfigurando).
  • Is getting used to: Process of adaptation (Se está acostumbrando a).
  • Got accepted: Passive outcome (Fue aceptado).
